## Task 3
The Vertical Game: MATE Floats! 70 points. Design and construct an operational vertical profiling float.

- **Game Plan – 5 Points** 
    | Task | Points |
    |------|--------|
    | Design, build, and submit DOC-004 for a vertical profiling float with a buoyancy engine| 5 |
- **Mic'd Up – 5 Points**
    | Task | Points |
    |------|--------|
    | Float transmits at least one defined data packet to the shore station before descending | 5 |
- **Vertical Profile 1 - 25 Points, -5 penalty**
    | Task | Points |
    |---|---|
    | Complete vertical profile via buoyancy engine | 10 |
    | Transmit ≥10 data packets to station | 5 |
    | Data confirms 2.0 m depth held for 30 sec (7 packets, 5 sec apart, within 1.67–2.33 m) | 5 |
    | Temp & light readings within range every 0.5 m on ascent (4.0 m → 0.0 m) | 5 |
     - **Penalty:** Float touches pool bottom 
- **Vertical Profile 2 - 25 Points, -5 penalty** 
    - Same as above
- **Review the Play - 10 Points**
    | Task | Points |
    |------|--------|
    | Graph **temperature vs. depth** (temp on X-axis, depth on Y-axis) | 5 |
    | Graph **light vs. depth** (light on X-axis, depth on Y-axis) | 5 |
    - Must be done on a computer; no hand-drawn graphs
    - If float fails to collect data, MATE-supplied data may be used instead (forfeits communication/sensor points)

- At the World Championship the float runs at the same time as the ROV tasks, in a separate area of the diving well, with two extra team members. Bring the separate MATE Floats! score sheets from the check-in folder. AC power is available there, compressed air is not.
- The float is hand launched and counts as deployed once nobody touches it and it floats on the surface.
- The company builds both the transmitter on the float and the receiver at the station. Other companies transmit at the same time, and the receiver should show nothing but the float's packets.
- A buoyancy engine moves fluid between the inside and outside of the float to change its density. Motors moving air or liquid count. Propellers and water jets do not.
- Completing a profile means breaking the surface, reaching 4.0 m or deeper, and breaking the surface again. A float that hits the bottom still completes the profile, with the penalty. There is one penalty per profile.
- The 2.0 m hold is measured at the top of the float, ±33 cm. Leaving the range restarts the 30 seconds. If the depth sensor is lower on the float, tell the judge the adjusted range before deployment, for example 1.92 to 2.58 m for a sensor 25 cm below the top.
- Each of the nine ascent readings is within 20 cm of its depth with a temperature within 2.0 °C of the true temperature. Light has no range. MATE has a reference temperature readout in the practice area.
- A profile that misses points may be made up by a later one. No more than two profiles earn completion points. Tell the judge and show the data.
- Graph one profile with data from every required depth, during the 15 minute run. Data may be typed or pasted in by hand.
- Requesting MATE's data forfeits the points for transmitting profile packets, sensor readings and graphing the float's own data. Completing profiles and holding depth still score if the data shows it. Graphing MATE's data is worth the same 10 points.
- The World Championship pool is 5.18 m deep. Regionals in shallower pools scale the depths, for example hold at 1.0 m and descend to 2.0 m in a 3 m pool.

---

### Vertical Profile Sequence
1. Surface → descend to **2.0 m**, hold for **30 seconds**
2. Descend to **4.0 m**
3. Ascend to surface, collecting data every **0.5 m** (±20 cm tolerance per depth)

### Required Data Packet Fields
- Company number, provided by MATE a few weeks before the competition
- Time (UTC, local, or float time)
- Pressure (Pa or kPa) and/or depth (m or cm)
- Temperature (°C)
- Light (any appropriate unit: lux, W/m², V, etc.)
- Anything else the company needs for the task

Pressure or depth, temperature and light all correlate to the transmitted time. MATE's example: `EX01 1:51:42 UTC 9.8 kpa 1.00 meters 23.6°C 0.546 V`.

### Cameras and Control Station
- Control must be computer or electronic based. Switch box controllers are not permitted.
- USB cameras plugged directly into a laptop are not allowed. A USB extender powered at the ROV end is acceptable.
- Pilots are penalized for looking into the pool. The station is set up so the ROV is only visible through its cameras.
- At the World Championship, bring an HDMI splitter at the control station so MATE can put the ROV camera view on the livestream. A split of the GUI or other displays may also be provided. MATE supplies an HDMI connection box and a 2 m cable at the station, connected during setup. The pilot's feed must not be interrupted by the split.
- The ROV and control system may not broadcast video or other data to anyone outside the product demonstration area. Doing so is disqualification.
- Any number of displays. They run off the AC outlet or their own batteries, must be secured, and may not have glass faceplates.
- Wireless controllers on their own battery are permitted at the company's risk. MATE will not stop the clock for interference.
- Monitors and joysticks/controllers may be shared between companies. Control systems and payload tools may not.
- Connections in the control box are covered to IP-30 (under 2.5 mm) whenever it is powered. 120 VAC wiring is separated and identified from DC, with a sign naming the wiring standard. Wires entering the box need strain and abrasion relief. A box with wires over an open lid needs a mechanism that locks it open.
- Strain relief at both tether ends uses a purpose-built connector. The ROV can be lifted by its tether.

### Vertical Profiling Float
The float is the only non-ROV device in 2027.

| Spec | Requirement |
|------|-------------|
| Size | Under 60 cm tall including any antenna, and under 18 cm in diameter, length and width, for the whole mission (new in 2027). No compartments that separate and nothing raised or lowered beyond that |
| Independence | No tether, airline or line to the surface or bottom. Any air is stored on the float. The ROV may not interact with it |
| Power | Onboard batteries only, 12 V and 5 A maximum, all through one fuse. Small button batteries may power timing devices |
| Prohibited | Thrusters and cameras |
| Batteries | NiMH (9 V, AA, C, D or 12 V brick) or AGM 12 V only. No alkaline, LiPo or outdoor rechargeables. Mounted so they are not loose. At most eight 1.2 V NiMH cells in series |
| Main fuse | 5 A maximum, within 5 cm of the battery positive terminal, visible without uncovering it. ATO, MINI blade (1 to 5 A) or cartridge fuses rated 32 VDC or more |
| Multiple packs | Joined at the negative terminals with the main fuse off the common negative. Each pack also fused to its own limit |
| FLA | Measured in waiting mode (motors off) and buoyancy change mode (motors on). Pick the standard fuse closest to it |
| Pressure relief | A pop-off end cap with a sealing diameter of 2.5 cm or more, not clamped, or (new in 2027) an approved off-the-shelf relief valve set to 15 PSI or less. A rubber stopper is no longer a primary method |

| Battery Type | Maximum Fuse Size |
|---|---|
| NiMH AA | 2.0 A |
| NiMH C or D | 5.0 A |
| NiMH 9-volt | 200 mA |
| NiMH (brick) / AGM 12 V | 5.0 A |

A NiMH cell's maximum current is its mAh rating divided by 1000. Parallel strings multiply the limit, up to 5 A.

DOC-004, the float design document, is 2 pages plus a 1 page SID with the fuse symbol, FLA in water and fuse size. It must contain:
- A photo or diagram of the float
- The battery type and a photo of the battery pack
- A photo of the fuses
- FLA for waiting mode and buoyancy change mode, with a photo of the meter in buoyancy change mode (new in 2027)
- The buoyancy engine
- How the float communicates with the shore station, including any commands sent after deployment
- How the battery pack meets the float's current and voltage needs
- The pressure relief mechanism (new in 2027)
- The temperature and light sensors. For light, the sensor type, the quantity measured and the units reported (new in 2027)

### Fluid Power
Fluid power is anything using an external power source to make pressure, on the ROV or the surface. Syringes are not fluid power. A manual pump pressurizing a closed system is.

| Spec | Hydraulic | Pneumatic |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| Fluid | Water | Compressed air or inert gas |
| Maximum pressure | 10.33 bar (150 psig) | 2.75 bar (40 psig) |
| Component rating | 2x supply pressure | 2.5x supply pressure |
| Supply | Pump with relief valve (300 psig max) before a regulator (150 psig max) | MATE air at the World Championship, ¼ inch NPT male, hose may be 2 m away |

- No SCUBA tanks or chemical gas generation.
- Unpressurized containers need a ¼ inch hole in the bottom.
- Accumulators up to 1.25 L total at the allowed pressure.
- Soft pressure operated devices need a 12 psi relief valve, with specs sent to MATE by May 15, 2027.
- The fluid power quiz must be passed at 100% by April 30, 2027.

## Size and Weight Points
At the World Championship, size and weight are measured before every product demonstration run. Arrive in the on-deck circle 20 to 30 minutes early. Mission time starts even if measurement is not finished.

Size is length + width + height of the ROV with all tools and components, measured between flat planes touching its outermost parts. Neither measurement includes the tether. Weight also excludes the topside control system, the vertical profiling float and any independent sensors removable from the ROV.

| Size | Points |
|------|--------|
| < 120 cm | +30 |
| 120.1 - 140 cm | +10 |
| 140.1 - 160 cm | +5 |
| > 160.1 cm | 0 |

| Weight | Points |
|--------|--------|
| < 8 kg | +30 |
| 8.01 - 12 kg | +10 |
| 12.01 - 18 kg | +5 |
| 18.01 - 22 kg | 0 |

- Any ROV with one dimension over 1 m will not be allowed to compete
- Any ROV over 22 kg will not be allowed to compete
- Every tool used in the run must be measured. Detached tools may sit beside, on or inside the frame and are reattached during setup. Equipment not measured may not be used
- A solid tether attachment on the frame counts toward size. Flexible mesh strain relief not fixed to the frame does not

## Safety and Workmanship
- The safety inspection is pass/fail with three attempts. Failing the third disqualifies the company from the product demonstration.
- The workmanship inspection is worth 50 points, with 5 to 10 deducted per issue. It happens once, combined with the first safety inspection.
- At the World Championship, the first inspection is immediately after check-in on June 22 or 23. Missing it fails the first safety inspection and loses all 50 workmanship points.
- Safety must be passed before opening ceremonies on Day 1 (new in 2027).
- Every item used on the ROV must pass inspection, or the company is disqualified.
- The initial safety and documentation review of the submitted documents is worth 20 points.
